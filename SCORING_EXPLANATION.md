# How Resume Scores Are Calculated

## Overview

Resume scores are calculated **entirely by AI models** (OpenAI GPT-4, Anthropic Claude, or Google Gemini) based on comprehensive analysis of your resume content. The scoring is **subjective and AI-driven**, meaning each AI provider may score the same resume slightly differently based on their training and interpretation.

## Score Range

- **Scale:** 60-100 points
- **Minimum:** 60 (resumes that need significant improvement)
- **Maximum:** 100 (excellent, top-tier resumes)

## Score Categories

The prompt instructs AI models to use these general guidelines:

- **85-95:** Excellent (top performers)
- **75-84:** Good (above average)
- **65-74:** Average (needs improvement)
- **60-64:** Needs significant improvement

## Current Scoring Method

### How It Works

1. **AI receives the prompt** with:
   - Your resume text
   - Target job title
   - Instructions to score between 60-100
   - General score category guidelines

2. **AI analyzes the resume** holistically considering:
   - Content quality and relevance
   - Skills alignment with job requirements
   - Formatting and structure
   - Market competitiveness

3. **AI assigns a score** based on its overall assessment

4. **Backend validates** the score is between 60-100

### Important Note

**The scoring is NOT based on a fixed formula.** The AI models use their training and judgment to evaluate the resume. This means:
- Different AI providers may score the same resume differently
- Scores are more like "expert opinions" than calculated values
- The specific improvement suggestions are often more valuable than the numeric score

## What the README Says vs. Reality

The README mentions a specific weighting:
- Content Quality: 40%
- Skills Alignment: 30%
- Formatting & Structure: 20%
- Market Competitiveness: 10%

**However, this weighting is NOT explicitly sent to the AI models in the current prompt.** The AI models use their own judgment to weight these factors, which is why:
- Different AI providers may score the same resume differently
- Scores can vary based on the AI's interpretation
- The scoring is more of an "expert opinion" than a strict formula

## Why Scores Differ Between Providers

When using multi-AI comparison, you may see different scores from different providers because:

1. **Different Training Data:** Each AI model was trained on different datasets
2. **Different Algorithms:** Each provider uses different underlying technology
3. **Different Interpretations:** Each AI may emphasize different aspects
4. **No Standardized Formula:** There's no fixed calculation - it's AI judgment

## Industry Benchmarks

The AI also provides:
- **Average Score:** Typical score for resumes in this industry (usually ~75)
- **Top Performers Score:** Score of top-tier candidates (usually ~90)
- **Your Score:** The individual assessment

These benchmarks are also **AI-generated estimates**, not based on actual historical data.

## Limitations

1. **No Standardized Formula:** Scores are AI opinions, not calculated formulas
2. **Subjective:** Different AIs may score differently
3. **No Historical Data:** Benchmarks are AI estimates, not real statistics
4. **Context-Dependent:** Scores are relative to the job title provided

## Improving Score Accuracy

To get more consistent and accurate scores:

1. **Use Multi-AI Comparison:** Compare scores from multiple providers
2. **Look at Consensus:** If multiple AIs agree, it's more reliable
3. **Focus on Improvements:** The specific improvement suggestions are often more valuable than the score itself
4. **Consider the Range:** A score of 75 vs 80 is less meaningful than the specific feedback

---

**Bottom Line:** The current scoring is AI-driven expert opinion, not a mathematical formula. The value is in the comprehensive feedback and improvement suggestions, not just the numeric score.
